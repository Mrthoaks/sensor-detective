👩‍🏫 Teacher guide: how this game follows the 5E model
StageTimeWhat students doWhy it works
🎯 Engage5–7 minSee a storm scene where the normal photo fails but another sensor still works. Commit to predictions about what satellites can and cannot see.Creates a puzzle and surfaces prior ideas and misconceptions before instruction. Predictions are revisited in Evaluate.
🔎 Explore15–20 minInvestigate six unnamed sensors over one landscape. Toggle night and clouds, probe surfaces, solve a mission per sensor, then run the stress test.Inquiry before terminology: students find the patterns (vegetation, moisture, heat, fingerprints, all-weather, height) first.
💡 Explain10–15 minUnmask each mystery sensor by naming it, then read the formal concepts: wavelength, passive vs active, detection, example sensors, limitations.Scientific vocabulary is attached to experiences students already had, which strengthens retention.
🧭 Elaborate12–15 minDispatch the right imagery to eight real problems, some needing two types combined.Transfer to new contexts and justification of choices. Good for small-group discussion before checking.
🏆 Evaluate10 minQuiz, written explanation, prediction revisit (then vs now), self-rating, certificate.Retrieval practice plus metacognition. The results screen shows each student what to review.

Stages unlock in order for students. Progress is saved in this browser. Projecting for a whole class?

The scene and readouts are simplified, illustrative values, not real measurements. For real imagery, pair the lesson with NASA Worldview, USGS EarthExplorer, or the Copernicus Browser.
